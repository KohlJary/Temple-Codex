**Verdict: Supported, with a slightly narrower scope than the headline.** The retry fixes the flakiness in `test_upload_large` as measured. The evidence is better than your hedging suggests, and the idea isn't dumb.

**What I checked (read, not executed):**
- `retry.diff` is the only change. It retries `put` up to 3 times on `requests.exceptions.ConnectionError`, with 0.5s and 1s backoff, and re-raises on the last attempt.
- `runs/before.txt` shows 17 failures in 200 runs (8.5%). All 17 are the same error: `ConnectionResetError(104)` at `uploader/client.py:15`, which is `upload_chunk`. That is the exact exception type the retry catches.
- `runs/after.txt` shows 0 failures in 200 runs.
- Fisher exact test on 17/200 vs 0/200 gives p of about 1e-5 (I ran this). Noise doesn't explain the gap.
- Your design is sound. The runs were interleaved in batches of 20 on the same machine, so time-of-day network quality doesn't confound the comparison. The diff was the only change.
- The mechanism is confirmed by your debug counter, not just inferred. In the "after" runs, 19 runs hit a connection reset and recovered on retry. 19 is close to the 17 failures you saw without the retry. So the retry is absorbing the same resets that were failing the test, rather than the test getting lucky.

**Narrower wording I'd stand behind:**
"With `retry.diff`, `test_upload_large` passed 200/200 on a1b2c3d, versus 183/200 without it. Every baseline failure was a connection reset, which the retry now recovers from. The underlying resets still happen in about 10% of runs (19/200); the retry hides them."

Three limits on "fixes":
1. **Root cause not addressed.** The resets still occur (19 of 200 runs). If the cause is the test server, a proxy, or the keep-alive setup, the retry is a mitigation and the test is passing around a real problem. Whether that matters depends on what you mean by "fixes the flakiness". If you mean "the test goes green", yes. If you mean "uploads no longer get reset", no.
2. **A zero is not a guarantee.** 0/200 means the true failure rate is probably under about 1.5% (rule of three). It does not mean 0. One run needed all 3 attempts, so a three-reset run, which would fail, is plausible at a low rate. "Fixed" is fine for CI purposes, but I wouldn't say "can't flake".
3. **Retry safety in production code.** I only looked at the diff, not the callers. Retrying `put` is fine if `data` is bytes. If any caller passes a file-like stream, a retry would resend from the wrong offset and could corrupt the upload. That is outside what your test shows, so please check before shipping.

**What I did not examine or run:**
- I couldn't re-run the test. I only had the notes, the diff, and the two logs, with no repo or commit a1b2c3d. So the 183/200 and 200/200 numbers are your logs, not numbers I reproduced.
- I haven't seen the debug counter or the batch-interleaving script, so I'm trusting that the alternation happened as described. The logs are consistent with it.
- I haven't checked whether the `time.sleep` backoff slows the test or the real upload path.

**To reach a plain "Supported":** confirm that no caller passes a stream to `upload_chunk`, and say in the commit message that the retry masks resets rather than removing them. Optionally, one batch with a failure-injecting server would confirm that the third attempt behaves correctly.
