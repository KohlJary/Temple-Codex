Hi, and thanks for the kind words. Good news: it's a one-character fix, and you've found out the night before the review rather than after release.

**Verdict on the 3.2.0 entry ("Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes. Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`."): Contradicted. Don't ship it as written.**

**The bug.** `auth.py` line 10 already defines `TOKEN_TTL = 15 * 60`, which is 900 seconds. `is_expired` (line 22) then compares against `TOKEN_TTL * 60`, which is 54,000 seconds. Tokens actually live 15 hours, not 15 minutes. That is 60x too long, in the one place a security feature must be right.

**What I ran.** I imported `auth.py` and checked a token created at t=0:
- 14 min: not expired (correct)
- 16 min: not expired (should be expired)
- 60 min: not expired (should be expired)
- 840 min (14 h): not expired (should be expired)
- 960 min (16 h): expired

So the real cutoff is 15 hours.

**Why the tests pass anyway.** I ran `pytest tests/`, and both tests pass (2 passed). They only check a token 60 seconds old (valid) and one 2 days old (rejected). Both are far from the 15-minute boundary, so they pass at any TTL between about 1 minute and 2 days. The "Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`" part of the entry is therefore also not supported. The tests cover "fresh is valid, ancient is rejected" and say nothing about 15 minutes. I did not mutate the code to confirm this, but the 15-hour behavior is already proof they can't tell.

**Fix.**
1. In `auth.py`, change `TOKEN_TTL * 60` to `TOKEN_TTL`.
2. Add boundary tests, for example:
   - `now = created_at + TOKEN_TTL - 1` is not expired
   - `now = created_at + TOKEN_TTL + 1` is expired and `redeem` raises `PermissionError`
   - 16 minutes is rejected (this one fails today and passes after the fix)
3. Then the changelog line is true as written, and the "Covered by" part is too. I'm happy to re-review once it's changed. If the boundary tests fail before the fix and pass after, I'll grant Supported.

**Not examined.** Only these three files. I didn't look at where tokens are created or delivered, whether they're single-use, or anything else the feature touches. The one-use and storage side of a reset flow is worth a look from your lead, but that's outside what I checked.

You caught this in time, and it's a very common mistake (a unit conversion applied twice). Good luck tomorrow.
