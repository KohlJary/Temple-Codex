**Verdict: Supported, with a narrower scope.** It's not dumb. Your experiment is well designed and the data back the claim as long as you word it a bit more narrowly (rewrite below).

**What the logs show (I read them, and ran one calculation on the counts):**
- Without the retry: 17 of 200 runs failed (8.5%). With only retry.diff: 0 of 200 failed. Fisher exact p is about 5e-6, so this is not noise.
- Every one of the 17 failures is the same exception: `ConnectionError` (connection reset by peer) at `uploader/client.py:15`. That is the `put` line inside `upload_chunk`, and it is exactly the exception the diff catches. The "after" log says no other exception types appeared.
- The retry counter in after.txt fits the failure rate. 19 of 200 runs hit a reset (9.5%), against 8.5% failing before. 18 recovered on attempt 2 and 1 on attempt 3. So the passes are retries absorbing resets, not some other change that happened to make the test pass.
- Your design handles the usual confounds: same commit, same machine, interleaved batches of 20, and a failure signature that matches the mechanism. A time-of-day or quiet-network explanation is unlikely.

**Narrower wording I'd stand behind:** "Adding the retry in retry.diff eliminated the connection-reset failures in test_upload_large (17/200 before, 0/200 after, interleaved runs on one machine). The retry masks the resets rather than removing their cause."

**Things to keep in mind, in order of importance:**
1. **"Fixes the flakiness" can mean two things.** The test now passes. The cause of the resets (server, proxy, or network dropping the connection) is untouched. If the resets point to a real server-side bug, the retry hides it. That is a legitimate fix for the client, but say so in the commit message.
2. **One environment.** Everything ran on one machine, one afternoon, against whatever endpoint the test uses. I can't say the rate or the fix transfers to CI or other networks.
3. **Retry budget.** Using your observed rate, three straight failures would be expected in roughly 0.2 of 200 runs, which is under one run. That makes 0 of 200 unsurprising, but residual flakiness at around 0.1% per run is possible, and 200 runs can't rule it out. Only `ConnectionError` is retried, so a `Timeout` would still fail the test. None was seen.
4. **Minor:** the after runs included a debug counter you removed before committing. That is harmless as far as I can tell, but "ONLY retry.diff" is not strictly literal.

**What I did not do:** I didn't have the repo, the test, or the endpoint, so I did not rerun the test or break the retry to see whether the test notices. The pass/fail counts come from your logs and I took them as recorded. I also didn't check that retrying `put` is safe against your server. Re-sending a chunk is fine if the PUT is idempotent, which I'd expect but did not verify.

If you want to take it from "supported by logs" to "reproduced," rerun a batch yourself on a different machine or in CI. I'd consider that optional given how clean this result is.
