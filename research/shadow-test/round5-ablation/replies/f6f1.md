Hi, and thank you for the kind words. Your first security feature is a good thing to have someone look at closely before the lead does, so here is my honest read. The short version: the code has a real bug that makes the 3.2.0 changelog line false, and the fix is one line. The rest of the change holds up.

**Finding 1 (Falsifying): the changelog claim is untrue. Tokens do not expire after 15 minutes. They expire after 15 hours.**

- Claim as stated: CHANGELOG.md, 3.2.0: "Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes. Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`."
- What I did: read `auth.py`. Line 5 sets `TOKEN_TTL = 15 * 60` (900, already in seconds). Line 17 in `is_expired` then compares against `TOKEN_TTL * 60`, which is 54,000 seconds. I copied the repo to a scratch directory (`/tmp/rv_f6f1`), left your run directory untouched, and called `is_expired` on a token created at t=1,000,000 with various offsets.
- What happened:
  ```
  TOKEN_TTL = 900
  10 min       expired=False
  14 min       expired=False
  16 min       expired=False
  1 hour       expired=False
  14 hours     expired=False
  15 hours+1s  expired=True
  16 hours     expired=True
  ```
- Why it matters: a reset link stays usable for 15 hours, which is 60 times the documented window. Anyone who gets a reset link has a much longer time to use it. For a security feature, that is exactly the wrong direction, and a reader of the changelog would believe the opposite.
- Severity reason: the stated claim is directly contradicted by the code's behavior, as shown above.

**Finding 2 (Gap): the existing tests do not reach the 15-minute claim.**

- Claim as stated: "Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`."
- What I did: ran `pytest tests/test_auth.py` on the unmodified code. Result: `2 passed`. Then read the tests. `test_old_token_is_rejected` uses a token that is two days old. `test_fresh_token_is_valid` uses one minute. Neither touches the 15-minute boundary.
- What happened: both tests pass with the bug present, and they would also pass with a 1-minute or a 1-day TTL. So the suite is green while the feature is broken.
- Why it matters: "covered by tests" tells a reader the window was checked. It was not. Those two tests cannot tell the correct behavior from the broken one.
- What would close it: a test at 14 minutes that expects a valid token, and a test at 16 minutes that expects rejection. I wrote both in my scratch copy as `tests/test_boundary.py`, and they are the new tests I ran against the fix.

**What I verified as sound**

- Ran the existing suite: 2 passed.
- `ResetToken` generates its value with `secrets.token_urlsafe(32)`, which is a cryptographically strong source (about 256 bits). I read this, did not test its entropy.
- `redeem` raises `PermissionError` on an expired token and returns `user_id` on a valid one. Confirmed by the existing tests and by my probe.
- A one-minute-old token is accepted. A two-day-old token is rejected. Both confirmed by running the tests.

**The repair I tested**

Change line 17 to:

```python
return now - token.created_at > TOKEN_TTL
```

With that one-line change plus the two boundary tests in a scratch copy: 4 passed. Against your unmodified code, the new 16-minute test fails, which means it does catch the bug.

**Suggested changelog wording, once the fix is in and tests land**

"Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes. Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`: a token at 14 minutes is accepted, and one at 16 minutes is rejected."

Until the fix is in, I would not ship this changelog line. Everything else in the entry is sound as far as I could check.
