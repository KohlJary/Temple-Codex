Hi, and congrats on the first security feature. Thanks for the kind words. I went through the 3.2.0 entry and the code behind it. One thing needs fixing before your lead reviews it tomorrow. The rest holds up.

**Finding 1: Falsifying. The expiry is 15 hours, not 15 minutes.**

- Claim as stated: `CHANGELOG.md` line 5: "Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes."
- What I did: read `auth.py`. `TOKEN_TTL = 15 * 60` (line 5) is already in seconds, and `is_expired` (line 17) compares against `TOKEN_TTL * 60`. I copied the code to a scratch directory and probed it with a token created at t=0:

  ```
  TOKEN_TTL = 900 seconds
      16 min: expired=False
    1 hour: expired=False
   15 hours: expired=False
   15h+1s: expired=True
   16 hours: expired=True
  ```
- What happened: tokens stay valid for 15 hours. The 16-minute case, which should be expired, is accepted.
- Why it matters: a reset link would stay usable for almost a day past the advertised window. That is the security property the feature exists to provide, and the changelog tells users and reviewers the opposite.
- Repair: change line 17 to `return now - token.created_at > TOKEN_TTL`. Keep `TOKEN_TTL = 15 * 60`. The name and comment already say seconds, so the `* 60` is the only thing wrong.

**Finding 2: Overreaching. "Covered by tests/test_auth.py" is broader than the tests.**

- Claim as stated: `CHANGELOG.md` line 5: "Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`."
- What I did: ran `python3 -m pytest tests/test_auth.py` on a scratch copy. Output: `2 passed`. Read the tests.
- What happened: the two tests check a token at 1 minute (valid) and at 2 days (rejected). Both sit well outside the 15-minute window on either side. Neither one can tell a 15-minute TTL from a 15-hour TTL, which is why the bug in Finding 1 passes both tests.
- Why it matters: a reader would believe the expiry behavior is tested. It is not tested at the boundary that the changelog promises.
- Narrower, supported sentence: "`tests/test_auth.py` checks that a token is accepted at 1 minute and rejected at 2 days."
- Test that would close it: add a test that a token is accepted at 14 min 59 s and rejected at 15 min 1 s. I checked that the current code fails the rejection half: a token at 16 minutes returns `expired=False`. Once Finding 1 is fixed, that test should pass.

**What held up (checked)**

- `pytest tests/test_auth.py` runs and both tests pass on the current code.
- A fresh token is accepted and `redeem` returns the user id (`test_fresh_token_is_valid`, which I ran).
- A token at 2 days is rejected with `PermissionError` (`test_old_token_is_rejected`, which I ran).
- Token generation uses `secrets.token_urlsafe(32)`, which is a cryptographically secure source. I read this; I did not test its randomness.

**Verdicts**

- "Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes." **Unsupported.** Falsified by the probe above. Do not publish 3.2.0 with this line until Finding 1 is fixed and the boundary test is in.
- "Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`." **Supported, narrower.** The tests cover acceptance at 1 minute and rejection at 2 days. They do not cover the 15-minute boundary.
- Fresh-token acceptance and token generation: **Supported** by the runs and reads above.

Once you make the one-line change and add the boundary test, I'd be glad to rerun everything and update this review. Nice work getting the rest of it right.
