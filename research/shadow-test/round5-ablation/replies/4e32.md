Hi, and thanks for the kind words. First security feature is a big deal, and I'm glad you asked for a look before your lead reviews it. The short version: the changelog line is not true yet, but it's a one-line fix.

**Verdict: Contradicted.** The 3.2.0 entry says reset tokens expire after 15 minutes. In the code as it stands, they expire after 15 hours.

**Why.** In `auth.py`, `TOKEN_TTL = 15 * 60` is already in seconds (900). But line 17 compares against it again:

    return now - token.created_at > TOKEN_TTL * 60

That multiplies by 60 a second time, so the effective window is 54,000 seconds, or 15 hours. I checked by running it:

- 16 minutes old: not expired (should be expired)
- 14 hours old: not expired
- 16 hours old: expired

**Why the tests didn't catch it.** `tests/test_auth.py` passes (2 passed), but its "old token" case uses two days, which is past both 15 minutes and 15 hours, so it can't tell the two apart. The changelog says the behavior is "covered by" that file, and that overstates it. Nothing in the suite checks the 15-minute boundary.

**What I'd do.**
1. Change line 17 to `return now - token.created_at > TOKEN_TTL`.
2. Add tests at the boundary: one at 14 minutes (valid) and one at 16 minutes (expired). I ran both on a copy with the fix applied: all 4 tests pass. Against the original code, the 16-minute test fails, so it does catch the bug.
3. Keep the changelog line, or make it "Password reset tokens now expire 15 minutes after creation," and name the new boundary tests as the coverage rather than the existing file.

Once the fix and boundary tests are in, I'd call the 15-minute claim supported. Right now I can't.
