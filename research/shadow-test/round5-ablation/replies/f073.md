Hi, I went through NOTES.md, retry.diff, and the two run logs. Short answer: the claim holds in a narrower form. In these runs the retry eliminates the connection-reset failures of test_upload_large. I did not rerun pytest, because the repo and uploader/client.py are not in the run directory.

**Claim as stated** (NOTES.md line 3): "adding the retry in retry.diff fixes the flakiness in test_upload_large."

**Verdict: Supported, narrower.** The sentence the evidence carries: "On commit a1b2c3d, on one machine in one afternoon, adding retry.diff took test_upload_large from 17 failures in 200 interleaved runs to 0 in 200, and the retry recovered every connection reset seen in the retry batch."

## Findings

**1. Overreaching: "fixes the flakiness."**
- Claim as stated: NOTES.md line 3.
- What I did: read runs/before.txt and runs/after.txt, and read retry.diff.
- What happened: before.txt reports "passed: 183 / failed: 17", and all 17 failures are "ConnectionError ('Connection aborted.', ConnectionResetError(104, 'Connection reset by peer')) (uploader/client.py:15 upload_chunk)". after.txt reports "passed: 200 / failed: 0", "retries triggered: 19 runs", and "no other exception types seen".
- Why it matters: the retry absorbs resets. It does not show why the connection resets, and the logs only cover resets. retry.diff catches requests.exceptions.ConnectionError only, so a ReadTimeout (the call sets timeout=30) is not retried. I checked this by running the patched method against a fake session: a ReadTimeout was raised after one call. The logs show no timeouts, so timeouts are untested either way. A reader would wrongly conclude that flaky uploads are solved in general.
- Repair: use the narrower sentence above, and add "retries connection resets on the chunk PUT; read timeouts are not covered."

**2. Gap: the retry resends the same `data` object.**
- Claim: implicit in "fixes." NOTES does not address it.
- What I did: ran the patched upload_chunk, copied verbatim from the new side of retry.diff, against a fake session that consumes a file-like body on each put.
- What happened: body sizes per attempt were [8, 0]. The second attempt sent an empty body.
- Why it matters: if the real call site passes a file or generator as `data`, every retry after a reset uploads an empty or truncated chunk. A lenient server could still return 200, so the 200/200 count would not catch it unless the test checks content.
- Test that closes it: check the type of `data` at the call site in uploader/client.py. Bytes is safe; a file-like object is not. Then confirm that test_upload_large verifies the uploaded bytes (a checksum, or a server-side read). I could not check either, since neither file is in the run directory.

**3. Gap: "ONLY retry.diff applied" versus the debug counter.**
- Claim: NOTES.md line 6, "with ONLY retry.diff applied."
- What happened: after.txt says the retry counts came from "an added debug counter, removed before commit." That counter is not in retry.diff, and the logs do not say whether it was in the tree during the 200 after-runs.
- Why it matters: the counter is probably inert, but the claim says "ONLY," and the log suggests something else was applied.
- Test that closes it: state whether the counter was present during the after batches. If it was, rerun the 200 without it, or show that the counter is a pure increment with no effect on control flow.

**4. Gap: the line citation does not match the diff.**
- Claim: before.txt cites uploader/client.py:15 for the ConnectionError.
- What I did: read the hunk header in retry.diff, "@@ -12,5 +13,11 @@", and counted the old-side lines 12 to 16. They are: blank (12), `def upload_chunk` (13), `resp = self.session.put(...)` (14), `resp.raise_for_status()` (15), `return resp` (16).
- Why it matters: a ConnectionError raised by session.put would be reported at line 14. Line 15 is raise_for_status, which raises HTTPError, not ConnectionError. So either the before runs used a slightly different client.py, or the line number is off. If the trees differ, the comparison is less clean.
- Test that closes it: confirm the before and after runs used the same client.py. A diff of that file between the two trees would show it.

## What held up

- **Log arithmetic is consistent.** 183 + 17 = 200. The 17 listed run numbers (007 through 198) count to 17. 18 + 1 = 19 runs with retries, matching "19 runs."
- **Like-for-like failures.** All 17 before-failures are ConnectionResetError (errno 104) on the PUT path, and the retry covers that same exception class.
- **Interleaving as described.** Both logs say 10 batches of 20, interleaved, on a1b2c3d. NOTES.md says the same.
- **The quiet-network objection is answered.** The before group saw 17 reset events and the after group saw 19. The rates are similar, so the retry recovered resets the environment still produced, rather than the environment going quiet during the after batches. This is the strongest part of the case.
- **The difference is significant.** 17/200 against 0/200 gives a two-sided Fisher exact p of about 1.1e-5. The before failure rate is 8.5% (Wilson 95% CI 5.4% to 13.2%). The after result of 0/200 has a 95% upper bound of 1.9%.
- **The retry logic behaves as the diff says.** With a fake session, two resets followed by success took three calls, and three resets raised on the third call. That matches the one after-run that needed a third attempt.

## What I did not check

- I did not rerun the test. The repo and uploader/client.py are not in the run directory, and I wrote nothing there.
- Unverifiable by me: whether the real `data` argument is bytes (finding 2), and whether the before and after trees differed only by retry.diff (findings 3 and 4).

NOTES.md hedges with "not 100% sure." The logs support more than that hedge, but only at the narrower claim.

To make the claim survive as written, say: "eliminates connection-reset failures of test_upload_large in 200 interleaved runs on a1b2c3d," and answer the body-type and content-check questions in finding 2.
