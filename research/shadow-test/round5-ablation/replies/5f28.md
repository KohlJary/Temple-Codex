Verdict: Supported with narrower scope. The retry does what the logs say on this commit and machine. "Fixes the flakiness" goes further than the evidence.

Narrower claim that is true as far as I can check:
"On commit a1b2c3d, on one machine, adding the retry in retry.diff made tests/test_upload.py::test_upload_large pass 200/200 runs, versus 183/200 without it. All 17 failures without the retry were ConnectionError (connection reset by peer)."

What I checked:
- runs/before.txt: 183 passed, 17 failed, all 17 are requests.exceptions.ConnectionError (connection reset by peer).
- runs/after.txt: 200 passed, 0 failed. The debug counter reports retries in 19 runs, 18 succeeding on attempt 2 and 1 on attempt 3, with no other exception types.
- Fisher's exact test, 17/200 vs 0/200: two-sided p is about 1e-5. The difference is not noise at this sample size.
- Logic of retry.diff: I rebuilt the new upload_chunk from the diff in /tmp/rv_5f28 and ran four pytest cases against a mocked session. Two resets then success passes. Three resets raise. HTTPError is not retried. ReadTimeout is not retried. All pass. This checks the diff as written, not your repo.

Problems, most important first:
1. The traceback line number does not match the diff. Every before-failure reports uploader/client.py:15 in upload_chunk. From the hunk header (@@ -12,5), old line 14 is the session.put call and old line 15 is resp.raise_for_status(). A ConnectionError should come from line 14. Either the line numbers in the log are off, or the before-run was not on the exact tree of a1b2c3d. Please check the before-run's client.py against the commit and re-paste the traceback. I could not open the commit from my allowed scope, so I inferred this from the diff.
2. The "after" run was not exactly retry.diff alone. You added a debug counter and removed it before the commit. Confirm that the tree you ran matches the committed tree plus retry.diff with nothing else, or say what differed. The counter is probably harmless, but the claim says "ONLY retry.diff applied."
3. The logs are summaries. runs/ contains two summary files, not the per-run pytest output. The retry log has no run IDs, so I can confirm the counts are similar (19 retried vs 17 failed) but not that the same runs were the ones that needed a retry. Adding run IDs to the retry log and keeping the raw output would close this.
4. The root cause is unknown. "Connection reset by peer" could come from the server, a proxy, or the test fixture. If the test server resets connections on purpose, the test is measuring its own injected fault, and the retry hides that in production code. Nothing in the run folder says which it is. Worth a look before calling this a fix rather than a workaround.
5. Generalization is untested. One machine, one afternoon, one commit. The claim should not be stated for CI or other networks until those are run.
6. Retry safety is not checked. If `data` is a stream or file-like object, a retry sends an already-consumed body. I could not see the caller, so this is unverified. If `data` is bytes, it is fine.
