Hi! Happy to look, and congratulations on the first security feature. I found one real problem, and it is worth fixing before your lead sees it. It is a one-line fix.

**Claim as stated** (CHANGELOG.md, 3.2.0): "Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes. Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`."

**Finding 1: Falsifying. Tokens actually expire after 15 hours, not 15 minutes.**

- Why this level: the shipped behavior contradicts the changelog sentence by a factor of 60.
- What I did: read `auth.py` and ran it on a copy in /tmp/rv_6056, without touching your directory. I created a token at t=0 and called `is_expired` at several ages.
- What I found: `TOKEN_TTL = 15 * 60` (line 5) is already in seconds, with the comment "15 minutes, in seconds". Line 17 then computes `now - token.created_at > TOKEN_TTL * 60`, which multiplies by 60 a second time, giving 54,000 seconds.
- What happened (copied output):

```
14 min expired= False
16 min expired= False
60 min expired= False
840 min expired= False
900 min expired= False
901 min expired= True
960 min expired= True
```

- Why it matters: a reset token that leaks, for example through an email archive or a proxy log, stays usable for 15 hours. That is a much wider window than the 15 minutes the changelog promises. Anyone reading the changelog would believe the shorter window.
- Repair: on line 17, change `TOKEN_TTL * 60` to `TOKEN_TTL`.

**Finding 2: Gap. The tests cannot see this bug, so "Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`" overstates what they check.**

- Why this level: the claim may become true after the fix, but the current evidence does not reach it.
- What I did: ran `pytest` on a copy, and read both tests.
- What happened: `2 passed`. `test_fresh_token_is_valid` checks a token at 60 seconds old. `test_old_token_is_rejected` checks a token at 2 days old. Both results are the same whether the limit is 15 minutes or 15 hours, so the buggy code passes cleanly. Nothing tests the 15-minute boundary.
- Closing test: add two cases next to the existing ones. A token at 14 minutes should be valid, and a token at 16 minutes should be rejected. For example, `assert not is_expired(t, now=1_000_000 + 14*60)` and `assert is_expired(t, now=1_000_000 + 16*60)`. With the current code the second assertion fails. After the one-line fix, both pass. I did not run the fixed version in your tree, since I am not allowed to change it. I did confirm the buggy behavior above.

**What held up**

- `ResetToken` generates its value with `secrets.token_urlsafe(32)`, a sound choice of CSPRNG. I read this and did not run it.
- `redeem` calls `is_expired` before returning the user id, and raises `PermissionError` on expiry. I read this, and the 2-day test exercises the raise path.
- The fresh-token and old-token tests run and pass. They are fine as far as they go. They just do not reach the boundary.
- The `TOKEN_TTL` constant is clearly commented. The units mistake looks like a classic slip made by the constant and its use being a few lines apart, not a design problem.

**What I did not check**

- Whether anything else in the project (the email link, the database, the web layer) enforces its own expiry. Only `auth.py`, the tests, and the changelog exist in the directory, so I can only speak about those.
- Token reuse after a successful redeem. `redeem` does not invalidate the token, which may be handled elsewhere. This is a question, not a finding, since I cannot see the storage layer. It would be worth knowing the answer before your lead asks.

**Verdicts**

- "Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes": **Unsupported.** Actual expiry is 15 hours (901 minutes was the first rejected age in my run). After the one-line fix, the sentence would hold.
- "Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`": **Supported, narrower.** The tests cover "fresh tokens are accepted and 2-day-old tokens are rejected." They do not cover the 15-minute limit.
