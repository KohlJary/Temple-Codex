Not dumb at all. This is a well-built experiment, and the claim holds, with one wording change.

**Claim, restated narrowly**
"On commit a1b2c3d, on your machine and network that afternoon, applying only retry.diff took test_upload_large from 17/200 failures to 0/200."

**What held up (verified)**
- I read before.txt and counted the failure lines: 17 listed, matching "failed: 17". Every one is the same exception, ConnectionResetError(104) at uploader/client.py:15 in upload_chunk. That is one failure mode, and it is the one the diff catches.
- I ran Fisher's exact test on 17/200 vs 0/200 (computed, not estimated): p = 5.4e-6, one-sided. If the true failure rate were still 8.5% with the retry, 0 of 200 would have probability about 2e-8. So this is not luck.
- Your design is sound: same commit, only the diff changed, alternating batches of 20 so a quiet or noisy network period hits both arms, and a before arm that actually failed. Many people skip that last part.
- after.txt shows the mechanism, not just the outcome. Retries fired in 19 runs, all ConnectionError (connection reset), and all recovered (18 on the 2nd attempt, 1 on the 3rd). 19 runs needing a retry is close to the 17 failures you saw without it, which is what you would expect if the retry is absorbing exactly those resets.
- Reading retry.diff: the retry catches only ConnectionError, re-raises on the last attempt, and uses backoff of 0.5s then 1s. HTTP errors from raise_for_status still propagate. It is narrow, and it does not swallow everything.

**Findings**

1. Overreaching. "Fixes the flakiness."
   - Claim as stated: "the retry ... fixes the flaky upload test" (NOTES.md).
   - What I did: read before.txt, after.txt and retry.diff, and ran the Fisher computation above.
   - What happened: 0 failures in 200, but 19 of those 200 runs still hit a connection reset, which is now hidden.
   - Why it matters: the resets are not gone. The test now passes despite them. That is a fine fix for client resilience, but a reader may take it to mean the underlying reset (server or proxy side) is resolved. It is also ambiguous whether you meant the test or the product.
   - Supported sentence: "With the retry, test_upload_large passed 200/200, versus 183/200 without it. The failures were all connection resets, and the retry recovers from them. It does not stop the resets from happening."
   - Statistical limit: 0/200 bounds the true failure rate at about 1.5% (95% upper bound), not at zero.

2. Gap (small). The after run is not exactly "ONLY retry.diff".
   - after.txt says a debug counter was added and "removed before commit". So the 200 runs were retry.diff plus instrumentation. It is very unlikely to matter, but the notes say "ONLY". To close it, rerun 200 on the clean diff with no counter. I would withdraw this on a clean run with 0 or near-0 failures.

3. Note. I could not rerun anything. The repo and the a1b2c3d checkout are not in the folder, only the logs and the diff, so I cannot confirm the logs come from that commit or that the diff applies cleanly. I am taking the logs as reported. The per-batch breakdown is also not in the logs, so I cannot see whether failures were spread evenly over the 10 interleaved batches. If you have it, a quick look would show whether the before failures clustered in one bad network window, though 17 failures spread across run numbers 007 to 198 suggests they did not.

**Verdicts**
- "Retry.diff eliminates the test_upload_large failures seen at a1b2c3d (17/200 to 0/200)": Supported.
- "Retry.diff fixes the flakiness" as written: Supported, narrower. The sentence the evidence carries is the one in finding 1.
- "Only retry.diff was applied in the after arm": Supported, narrower: retry.diff plus a debug counter (finding 2).
- Whether the same holds on other machines, networks or commits: Unverifiable by me. I would need access to the repo and a run elsewhere; if it still fails 0 of N there, that settles it.

Nice work on the alternating batches and keeping the failure details in the logs.
