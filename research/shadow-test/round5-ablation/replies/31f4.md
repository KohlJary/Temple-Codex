**Verdict: Supported, with a narrower scope on one point.** Your claim holds up. It's not dumb. Retrying on ConnectionError removes the flakiness you measured in test_upload_large. The one thing I'd change is the wording, so it doesn't imply you fixed the cause of the resets.

**What I checked** (I read the files and ran one calculation; I did not re-run the test, because the repo and test aren't in the review folder):

- The design is a fair comparison. Same commit, only retry.diff applied, 200 runs each, interleaved in batches of 20 on the same machine. That rules out the quiet-network-time and different-commit explanations.
- Results: 17/200 failed without the retry and 0/200 with it. If retry had no effect, the chance of all 17 failures landing in the other arm is about 5e-6 (hypergeometric, computed). This is not noise.
- The failures are uniform. All 17 are the same `ConnectionResetError(104)` at `uploader/client.py:15` in `upload_chunk`, which is exactly the call the diff wraps and the exception it catches. So the fix targets the observed failure mode and not something nearby.
- The mechanism shows up in the after-log. Retries fired in 19 of 200 runs (18 recovered on attempt 2, 1 on attempt 3). That is close to the baseline failure rate (17 of 200, about 8.5%). In other words, the resets still happen at roughly the same rate, and the retry absorbs them. That is the strongest evidence in the notes, because it shows the zero is caused by the retry and not by the network being calmer.
- The diff matches what you describe. It retries 3 times with 0.5s and 1s backoff, re-raises on the last attempt, and catches only `ConnectionError`.

**Scope limits, in order of importance:**

1. **It masks the resets, it does not remove them.** The server or network still drops the connection about 8-9% of the time. "Fixes the flakiness in the test" is true. "Fixes the flaky upload" is not claimed, but don't let it be read that way. Suggested wording: "Retrying on ConnectionError (3 attempts, exponential backoff) eliminated test_upload_large failures: 0/200 vs 17/200 without it. The underlying connection resets still occur, in about 19/200 runs, and are now retried."
2. **0/200 is not 0%.** The 95% upper bound on the true failure rate is about 1.5%. Fine for "fixed in this environment," not for "never fails."
3. **Idempotency.** The retry re-sends a PUT of a chunk. That's normally safe, since PUT is idempotent. But if the server can apply a chunk and then reset before replying, a retry could double-apply. That is a production-behavior question the test doesn't cover. Worth a one-line check of the server contract before merging.
4. **Only ConnectionError is retried.** A ReadTimeout or a 5xx would still fail immediately. None appeared in your logs, so this is not a problem for the claim, just a note on coverage.

**Not examined:** the test and server code, whether the commit hash a1b2c3d is the one you ran (I took the logs at their word), other environments such as CI, and the removed debug counter. The logs are your own summaries and not raw pytest output; I did not see per-run timestamps confirming the interleaving.

**Bottom line:** Ship it with the narrower wording above. The only thing I'd want before treating it as done is a quick look at the idempotency of the chunk PUT.
