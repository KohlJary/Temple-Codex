Review of NOTES.md (d9c7). Short version: your hunch is right for the thing you tested, and your hedging is fair. Details below.

## Claim, restated narrowly
"On commit a1b2c3d, on your machine and network that afternoon, applying retry.diff cuts test_upload_large failures from 17/200 to 0/200, and the failures were connection resets that the retry absorbs."

I did not rerun anything. I have no repo, no test, and no network. I read NOTES.md, retry.diff, runs/before.txt and runs/after.txt, and computed one statistic. Everything below is a read of your logs, not a reproduction.

## What held up
- **The difference is not chance (computed).** 17/200 vs 0/200, two-sided Fisher exact p is about 1e-5 (I computed it, one line of Python). Interleaving 10 batches of 20 handles the "quiet network time" worry. Nice design for a phone note.
- **The failure mode in the logs matches the fix (read).** before.txt lists 17 failures, and all 17 are the same error: ConnectionError / ConnectionResetError(104) at client.py:15 in upload_chunk. The diff catches exactly requests.exceptions.ConnectionError. I counted 17 "run" lines, which matches "failed: 17".
- **The mechanism accounts for the numbers (read).** after.txt says retries fired in 19 runs, and all were connection resets. 19 resets in the patched arm against 17 failures in the unpatched arm is what you would expect if the same fault occurs at about 8-9% per run in both arms and the retry absorbs it. 18 of those recovered on attempt 2 and 1 on attempt 3. That is much stronger than a bare pass/fail count, because you can see the retry doing the work.
- **Isolation.** Only retry.diff was applied, and the diff is small and does what it appears to do (read, not executed).

## Findings

**1. Gap: the logs reach "fixes the flakiness in this test here", not "fixes the flakiness".**
- Claim as stated: "adding the retry in retry.diff fixes the flakiness in test_upload_large."
- What I did: read before.txt / after.txt.
- What happened: one machine, one afternoon, one network, one commit. All 17 failures are one error type.
- Why it matters: if CI fails for other reasons (timeouts, 5xx, a different reset pattern), this evidence does not speak to them. The retry also only catches ConnectionError, so a timeout or HTTPError would still fail the test.
- What would close it: run the same 200x comparison in CI, or wherever it flakes for others. If the failure rate there is also near zero with the patch, I'd call it fully supported.

**2. Gap: the test now passes, but the underlying reset still happens.**
- Claim as stated: "fixes the flakiness."
- What happened: after.txt shows the resets still occur in 19 of 200 runs. The retry hides them.
- Why it matters: that is a fine outcome for a client library, and it is what retries are for. But if the resets point to a server or proxy bug, the test no longer detects it. Worth one sentence in the PR so nobody thinks the cause is gone.

**3. Gap: retry safety depends on code I can't see.**
- What I read: diff lines in upload_chunk. The retry re-sends `data`. PUT is idempotent, so a repeat is fine. If `data` is ever a file-like stream rather than bytes, the second attempt would send an already-consumed body.
- I can't tell which it is from the diff alone. Check the call sites. If it is always bytes, this is a non-issue.

**4. Note (minor): the "after" arm ran with a debug counter that was removed before commit.** It is very unlikely to change behavior, but it is a small difference between what was measured and what would be committed. A final run of the committed diff with no counter would remove the question.

## Not a finding
Your NOTES.md calls itself messy and uncertain. The evidence is better than the tone suggests. "I think" undersells it; the over-hedging is the only mismatch I found between the text and the data.

## Verdict
- **Claim: retry.diff fixes the flakiness in test_upload_large.** Supported, narrower: "On a1b2c3d, in your environment, retry.diff took test_upload_large from 17/200 to 0/200 failures (p about 1e-5, interleaved batches); every observed failure was a connection reset, and the patched runs show 19 resets being retried successfully." The broader claim (all environments, root cause resolved) is not tested. Items 1 and 4 above are the runs that would close it.
