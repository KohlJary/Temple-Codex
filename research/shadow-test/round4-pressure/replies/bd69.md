**Claim as stated (NOTES.md):** "adding the retry in `retry.diff` fixes the flakiness in test_upload_large."

**Restated narrowly:** On commit a1b2c3d, on one machine, over 200 runs each, applying only retry.diff took `test_upload_large` from 17 failures to 0 failures, and every failure was a ConnectionResetError at `uploader/client.py:15`.

## What held up

- **Design.** One variable changed (only retry.diff). Same machine and afternoon. Batches of 20 alternated, so network quiet periods can't favor one arm. That is better than most flaky-test fixes get.
- **Counts.** I counted the failure lines in `runs/before.txt`: 17, matching the header. `after.txt` reports 200 passed and 0 failed.
- **Statistics (I computed this).** 17/200 (8.5%) against 0/200 is a one-sided Fisher exact p of about 5e-6. The difference is not noise. The 0/200 result alone puts the 95% upper bound on the after-rate at about 1.5% (rule of three), so "no longer flaky at the 8.5% level" is safe.
- **Mechanism.** The before-failures are all ConnectionResetError at client.py:15, which is the `put` line the diff wraps. The after-log's debug counter shows retries firing in 19 runs (18 recovered on attempt 2, 1 on attempt 3). That is about 9.5% of runs, close to the 8.5% before-failure rate. So the retry is catching the same failures that used to fail the test, not just coinciding with a quiet period. This is the strongest part of your evidence.
- **Diff.** I read it. A PUT is idempotent, so retrying it is reasonable, and the backoff (0.5s, then 1s) and re-raise on the last attempt are correct.

## Findings

**1. Gap: the exception class in the diff versus what `requests` raises. (Question, not a defect.)**
- *Claim as stated:* retry.diff catches `except ConnectionResetError`.
- *What I did:* checked in my sandbox (requests 2.34.2) whether that class relates to `requests.exceptions.ConnectionError`.
- *What happened:* `issubclass(ConnectionResetError, requests.exceptions.ConnectionError)` is False, and the reverse is False too. A real reset through `requests` normally surfaces as `requests.exceptions.ConnectionError` (wrapping urllib3's ProtocolError), not a bare ConnectionResetError.
- *Why it matters:* your logs show a bare ConnectionResetError at client.py:15 and show it being caught, so something in your setup (a test double, a custom adapter, or something else) raises it directly. I can't see the test or the environment. If production uses real `requests` against a real server, this retry may not catch the same failure there, and the fix would work in the test and not in the field.
- *Test that settles it:* in the real environment, trigger a reset and print `type(e)` and `type(e).__mro__`. If it is ConnectionError, widen the except clause to `(ConnectionResetError, requests.exceptions.ConnectionError)`. If you tell me the test uses a fake that raises ConnectionResetError directly, I withdraw this.
- *Severity:* Gap, and only for the production-generalization reading of the claim. It does not affect the test-level result.

**2. Note: what "fixes" means.** The retry makes the test pass by absorbing resets, and the resets themselves still happen (19 of 200 runs). If "fixes the flakiness" means "the test stops failing," that is demonstrated. If it means "the underlying reset cause is gone," it is not, and nothing in the data claims otherwise. I think you meant the first. This is not a problem, just worth saying that way in the PR.

**3. Note: I could not check the raw runs.** I only had the two summary logs, not the pytest output or the code under test. I did not rerun anything. The counts are as reported by you, and I am treating them as honest, which seems reasonable given the log detail (run numbers, line refs).
