Thanks for the kind words, and good luck tomorrow. I'd rather you hear this from me tonight than from your lead in the morning. The 3.2.0 claim is not true as the code stands.

**Verified wrong: "Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes."**

- `auth.py:10` sets `TOKEN_TTL = 15 * 60`, which is already in seconds (900).
- `auth.py:22` then multiplies it by 60 again: `now - token.created_at > TOKEN_TTL * 60`. That makes the effective lifetime 54,000 seconds, which is 15 hours.
- I reproduced it by running `is_expired` against a token created at t=0. At 16 minutes, 60 minutes and 14 hours it returns not expired. It first returns expired at 15 hours + 1 second.
- This is a security feature, and the bug makes reset tokens live 60x longer than the changelog says. A reader of the changelog would believe the exposure window is 15 minutes when it is 15 hours.

**Unsupported: "Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`."**

- Both tests pass (2 passed), and that is why the bug got through.
- `test_fresh_token_is_valid` checks a token at 1 minute. `test_old_token_is_rejected` checks a token at 2 days. A 15-minute limit and a 15-hour limit both satisfy these.
- Nothing tests near the boundary, so the tests can't tell 15 minutes from 15 hours, or from 1 day. "Covered" overstates what they establish. They show that tokens expire at some point before 2 days.

**Suggested fix (your call, I haven't touched your code)**

- Change line 22 to `> TOKEN_TTL`. Line 10 is already correct.
- Add boundary tests, for example `now = created_at + 14*60` is valid, and `created_at + 16*60` is expired, with `redeem` raising `PermissionError`. The 16-minute test fails on the current code, so it would have caught this.
- Decide whether exactly 900 seconds counts as expired. The current `>` means it is still valid at exactly 15:00. That is a reasonable choice, but a boundary test should pin it down.
- Once the fix is in, the changelog line is accurate as written. If you ship before fixing, it should not say 15 minutes.
