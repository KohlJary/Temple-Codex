Hi, and congratulations on your first security feature. I'm glad to look. There is one real problem, and it is worth fixing before your lead sees it tomorrow. It is a small fix.

The claim I examined: CHANGELOG.md, 3.2.0: "Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes. Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`."

Narrowed into two testable sentences:
1. A reset token is rejected once it is more than 15 minutes old.
2. `tests/test_auth.py` would fail if that were not true.

## Findings

### 1. Falsifying: tokens expire after 15 hours, not 15 minutes

- Claim as stated: "Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes" (CHANGELOG.md, 3.2.0).
- What I did: read `auth.py`, then ran `is_expired` in a scratch copy of the project. I did not touch your directory. The token was created at t=0, and I checked it at several ages.
- What happened:
  - `TOKEN_TTL = 15 * 60  # 15 minutes, in seconds` (auth.py line 5) is correct: 900 seconds.
  - `return now - token.created_at > TOKEN_TTL * 60` (auth.py line 17) multiplies by 60 a second time. The effective limit is 900 * 60 = 54,000 seconds, which is 15 hours.
  - Output, as age in seconds followed by `is_expired`: `901 False`, `3600 False`, `50400 False`, `54000 False`, `54001 True`.
  - So a token that is 14 hours old still redeems.
- Why it matters: the changelog promises a 15-minute window and the code allows 60 times that. For a password reset token, a longer window is a longer time for a leaked link or a stolen email to be useful. A reader would wrongly believe the exposure is 15 minutes.
- The likely repair is to drop the extra factor: `return now - token.created_at > TOKEN_TTL`. I have not applied it, since I can't modify your files. This is my suggestion from reading the code, not something I have run against your tree.

### 2. Gap: the cited test cannot detect this bug

- Claim as stated: "Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`."
- What I did: read both tests and ran `pytest` in the scratch copy.
- What happened: 2 passed. The tests check a 60-second-old token (valid) and a 2-day-old token (rejected). The 15-hour bug passes both, because 60 seconds is under either limit and 2 days is over either limit. Nothing probes the 15-minute boundary.
- Why it matters: the green tests are what make "covered" feel true. The bug above went through them unnoticed, which shows the changelog sentence overstates what the tests protect. Right now the tests cover "fresh is valid, ancient is rejected", not "15 minutes".
- What would close it: add boundary tests, for example (a) age `TOKEN_TTL - 1` is not expired, (b) age `TOKEN_TTL + 1` is expired and `redeem` raises `PermissionError`, (c) an age of 1 hour is expired. Test (c) fails on the current code and passes after the fix. If you add them, test (c) is your proof that the fix works. I have not run these, since I did not write them into your tree.

## What held up

- `TOKEN_TTL = 15 * 60` is 900 seconds, which is the right constant for 15 minutes. The intent is clear in the code. The slip is one extra `* 60` at the use site.
- Reran the test file: 2 passed, and both tests are valid for what they test. A 60-second-old token redeems and returns user_id 7, and a 2-day-old token raises `PermissionError("reset token expired")`.
- `redeem` correctly delegates to `is_expired` and raises on expiry. I did not find a problem there once the comparison is fixed.
- Tokens come from `secrets.token_urlsafe(32)`, the right primitive for this. I only looked at generation, not storage, delivery or single-use handling, which are not in this repo and are not claimed.
- Injecting `now` into `is_expired` and `redeem` is what makes the boundary tests easy to write.

## Not checked

The changelog claims only expiry, so I did not review whether tokens are single-use, rate-limited or stored hashed. Nothing in the project covers those. They are outside the claim, so I make no statement about them.

## Verdicts

- "Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes": **Unsupported.** The code expires them after 15 hours (the run above confirms 54,000 s).
- "Covered by `tests/test_auth.py`": **Unsupported.** The tests pass despite the bug and never probe the 15-minute boundary.

After the one-line fix and the boundary tests, I would expect both claims to hold. I'd be glad to re-check if you send it back. The timing is good: you found this the evening before review rather than after release.
