Short answer: the flakiness numbers may be real, but the retry as written can't be the thing that fixed them. The `except ConnectionResetError` never fires under `requests`, so the diff is effectively inert. I'd call the claim unverified, and I'd hold off on saying the retry fixes `test_upload_large`.

**What breaks it.** `retry.diff` catches `ConnectionResetError`. When I used `requests` 2.34.2 with urllib3 2.8.0 against a local server that resets the connection, `session.put` raised `requests.exceptions.ConnectionError` wrapping `ConnectionResetError(104)`. The raw `ConnectionResetError` is never raised, so the `except` doesn't match. I applied the diff's `upload_chunk` verbatim to a scratch `Uploader` (in `/tmp/rv_6e67/patched_client.py`) and ran it against a resetting server. It made one connection attempt and raised `ConnectionError` straight out. No retry happened.

**Why the logs don't add up.** `runs/after.txt` reports 19 retries, each logged as a `ConnectionResetError` on attempt 1 or 2. `runs/before.txt` reports 17 failures, each a raw `ConnectionResetError` at `uploader/client.py:15`. Under plain `requests`, neither can happen: the retry would never fire, and the failures would surface as `requests.exceptions.ConnectionError`. Either the uploader isn't a plain `requests` session, or the logs don't come from this code path. Until that's explained, I can't trust either log as evidence about this diff.

**What I checked, and how it went:**
- `runs/before.txt` vs `runs/after.txt`: 17/200 failed without the diff, 0/200 with it. A two-sided Fisher exact test on those counts gives p ≈ 1e-5 (scipy). The statistics are fine for the numbers as logged. The problem is what they measure.
- Confound: `after.txt` says "commit a1b2c3d + retry.diff only", but the retry log comes from "an added debug counter, removed before commit." If the counter was in the code during the 200 runs, the "after" build isn't diff-only. Say which it was.
- Interleaving in 10 batches of 20 is a reasonable control against drift. It doesn't rule out a changed network state across the afternoon, and it's one machine.
