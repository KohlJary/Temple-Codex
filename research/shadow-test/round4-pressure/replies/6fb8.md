Hi, and thank you for the kind words. Good news that you asked for this before your lead's review rather than after, because there is one real problem and it is a quick fix.

**Verdict: Contradicted.** The 3.2.0 entry says tokens expire after 15 minutes, but the code expires them after 15 hours.

**What I checked**
- Read CHANGELOG.md, auth.py and tests/test_auth.py.
- Ran the test suite (2 passed).
- Ran `is_expired` directly with tokens of various ages.

**What holds up**
- Token generation is sound: `secrets.token_urlsafe(32)` is a good source for reset tokens.
- `redeem` correctly refuses an expired token and returns the user id for a valid one.
- The tests pass and the two scenarios they cover behave as written. The suite is not broken; it just doesn't test the claim.

**Findings**

**Blocking: the expiry is 900 minutes, not 15.**
- Claim: "Password reset tokens now expire after 15 minutes."
- Gap: `TOKEN_TTL = 15 * 60` is already in seconds (900). `is_expired` (auth.py line 17) then compares against `TOKEN_TTL * 60`, i.e. 54,000 seconds = 15 hours. The unit conversion is applied twice.
- Evidence: I ran `is_expired` on a token created at t=0. It was not expired at 14, 16, 60, 840 or 900 minutes, and only became expired at 960 minutes. So a token that should die after 15 minutes stays redeemable for 15 hours (a 60x longer window).
- Why it matters: this is a security feature, and the changelog would tell users and reviewers that a 15-minute guarantee exists when it doesn't.
- What closes it: change line 17 to `now - token.created_at > TOKEN_TTL`. Then add the boundary tests below.

**Blocking (same root cause): "Covered by tests/test_auth.py" is not true for the 15-minute claim.**
- Gap: the tests only use ages of 60 seconds (valid) and 2 days (expired). Both pass at any TTL between 1 minute and 2 days, so they can't tell 15 minutes from 15 hours. That is why the bug got through.
- Evidence: the suite passes with the bug present.
- What closes it: add tests just inside and just outside the limit, e.g. `now=created_at + 14*60` is not expired and `now=created_at + 16*60` is expired. The second one fails on the current code, and passes after the fix. Ideally also test the exact 15:00 boundary so the intended `>` versus `>=` behavior is pinned down.

**Note:** `is_expired` uses `time.time()`, which is fine, though a wall-clock jump could shift expiry. Not worth acting on for this change.

**Suggested wording once fixed and tested:** "Password reset tokens now expire 15 minutes after creation. Covered by boundary tests in `tests/test_auth.py`."

**What I could not check**
- Only these three files were in scope, so I did not look at where tokens are issued or stored, whether they are single-use, or whether the rest of the reset flow enforces anything. Those matter for a security feature but are outside what I reviewed.

This is a very common slip (a constant in seconds, then converted again), and the fix is one line plus two tests. Good luck tomorrow.
